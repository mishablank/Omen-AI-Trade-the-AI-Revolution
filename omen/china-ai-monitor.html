Why this matters for US AI stocks

Enterprise switching is underway. CNBC (2026-07-07): Coinbase cut AI spend nearly 50% by moving ~1,200 internal agents to GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.7; Lindy migrated fully off Claude to DeepSeek; Uber capped engineer AI budgets at $1,500/month; Citi reportedly restricted access to the most expensive OpenAI/Anthropic tiers. Chinese models price 60–90% below US flagships for near-frontier quality.

The capex asymmetry is the exposure. US hyperscaler capex was ~$400B in 2025 with >$520B consensus for 2026, vs ~$57B for China's major platforms (UBS). If cheap open-weight models keep compressing inference pricing, the revenue side of that capex – and the margin structure of proprietary US model vendors – is what gets repriced. NVDA's China datacenter revenue already fell from ~$4.6B/yr to ~zero under export controls, so the remaining channel is margin pressure and demand-mix shifts, not lost China sales.

The counter-thesis is live too. 2026 has produced no repeat of the Jan-2025 DeepSeek selloff: NVDA beat earnings through DeepSeek V4's launch window and made new highs after V4-Pro. Chinese labs remain export-control constrained, and US frontier models still hold the #1 spots on quality leaderboards. Watch this page for the divergence, not the conclusion.

Methodology

Chinese AI Adoption Index = Σ(wi·si)/Σwi over available signal families, each normalized 0–100 against fixed reference ranges: router Chinese token share 0→70% (w=30; mean of the OpenRouter and Vercel-AI-Gateway normalized shares when both are available, OpenRouter alone otherwise), Hugging Face Chinese share of open-weight downloads 0→90% (w=20), GitHub star velocity across the flagship basket 0→300/day (w=15; the basket is rediscovered each run as the two most-starred repos per lab still pushed to within 180 days, so a new release enters it the week it lands), Ollama Chinese share of named-family pulls 0→70% (w=10), Western search/consumer interest share 0→50% (w=10), consumer-app Western top-chart presence 0→100 (basket-mean best top-100 rank, w=10), LMArena proximity 100−Elo-gap, floored at 0 (w=5). Weights follow the source note's suggested scheme, extended with the local-adoption family; excluded families renormalize. SPI = Chinese-lab ÷ US-lab routed tokens (named authors) on OpenRouter, trailing week. Histories: the updater appends one row per UTC day to china-metrics.csv (composite index, SPI, router/HF/Ollama Chinese shares, GitHub star velocity, frontier price gap) – the small history lines across the page read from it, so they are server-durable rather than per-browser; Polymarket odds histories come live from the public CLOB prices-history endpoint. Frontier price gap = median output $/M of US flagship lines (GPT top/mid, Claude Opus/Sonnet, Gemini Pro/Flash) ÷ median of Chinese lines (DeepSeek pro/flash, GLM, Kimi and Kimi Code, MiniMax, Qwen Max). Each line resolves to its newest live model in OpenRouter's catalogue at page load rather than to a pinned version, so a launch or a price cut moves the gap the day it ships and a retired slug surfaces as a named warning instead of quietly leaving the median. Lines are matched on name, never on price: Chinese labs deliberately price their newest flagship below their previous one, so ranking by cost would pick the older model. Supply-side panels (frontier gap, compute stock, chip supply, release cadence) are context, not index inputs – they carry no weight in the adoption index. Frontier gap = best AA Intelligence Index score per lab country, monthly since 2024; lag = months between a US model first reaching today's best Chinese score and the Chinese frontier getting there; near-frontier price ratio = cheapest blended $/M within 3 index points of each side's frontier, US ÷ Chinese.

Caveats

OpenRouter sees only routed traffic – it overstates Chinese share of the total market (first-party US API traffic is invisible) but is the best public real-time proxy for the substitutable segment; the Vercel AI Gateway cross-check (production apps, export cached 24h) partially corrects that bias and currently reads much lower. HF download totals mix model generations and exclude derivative quantizations. Ollama pulls are lifetime cumulative, so they lag shifts; the derived daily rate is the leading part. Fine-tune-tree counts depend on authors declaring base_model metadata. PyPI SDK installs undercount the Chinese side badly (OpenAI-compatible APIs ride the openai package). Leaderboard (LMArena official dataset, arena.ai scrape fallback), search interest (Google Trends), consumer-app chart presence (Apple RSS + Google Play), Vercel, Ollama, Kalshi, fine-tune trees and SDK installs are all refreshed by update-china-data.py; Artificial-Analysis scores refresh live when the updater has an ARTIFICIAL_ANALYSIS_API_KEY, else they remain a dated snapshot, and the Cloudflare-Radar consumer-traffic panel appears only when a CF_RADAR_TOKEN is configured (see freshness strip). Prediction-market prices are thin in places; Kalshi quotes are daily-batch, not live, and the discovered (AUTO) leadership rows rotate as each monthly cohort resolves, so that block is not a fixed panel across visits. The supply-side panels inherit their sources' blind spots: Epoch's cluster dataset covers an estimated 10–20% of world capacity and anonymizes Chinese systems (the split understates China); chip-sales figures are median estimates with wide confidence intervals, count designs rather than China's smuggled-in NVIDIA inflow, and lag ~2 quarters on the Chinese side; notable-model counts backfill for weeks after a release; the AA frontier series is rebuilt from the current catalogue, so delisted models silently leave its history, and it appears only when the updater has an ARTIFICIAL_ANALYSIS_API_KEY. Epoch CSVs are refetched weekly, not per-run. Nothing here is a validated forecast of equity returns – the companion monitor's own lead-lag test is honest about that.
