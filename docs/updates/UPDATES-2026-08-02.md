# 2026-08-02 – China monitor: three hardcoded baskets replaced with live discovery

Audit question: which data inputs on `/china-ai-monitor` are hardcoded but could be fed
from something that already updates itself?

Most of the page turned out to be genuinely live – `china-data.json` refreshes daily and
overrides nearly all of the embedded `SNAP` block. What was left hardcoded was not
*values* but **basket definitions**: hand-maintained lists of repos, model slugs and
market ids. The numbers computed from them were live; they were just being computed over
dead objects. All three are now rediscovered.

## 1. GitHub repo basket – was dragging the headline gauge

`GH_REPOS` pinned seven flagship repos. By August five of them had not been pushed to in
six months:

| repo | stars | last push |
|---|---|---|
| deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3 | 104,075 | 2025-08-28 |
| deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1 | 91,988 | 2025-06-27 |
| MiniMax-AI/MiniMax-M2 | 2,601 | 2025-11-13 |
| XiaomiMiMo/MiMo | 2,290 | 2025-06-05 |
| QwenLM/Qwen3 | 27,451 | 2026-01-09 |

`MoonshotAI/Kimi-K3` (7,880 stars, six days old) and `QwenLM/qwen-code` (26,514, pushed
daily) were not in it at all. The family read **3.9/100 on a 15% weight** – a measurement
artefact, not an adoption signal.

Recomputed from live data, the composite gauge is **48.0 ("Building")**, of which GitHub
contributes 0.59 points. Had that family read 60, the gauge would be 56.4 and the regime
chip would flip to **Displacing**. A stale list was holding the page's headline verdict
in place.

Now: `github_basket()` in `update-china-data.py` rediscovers, per lab org, the two
most-starred repos still pushed to within 180 days (`GH_ACTIVE_DAYS`), excluding
awesome-lists and cookbooks whose stars accrue on a completely different curve. Published
as `github.repos` in `china-data.json`; the page reads it and keeps `GH_SEED` only as a
fallback. First live run discovered 12 repos across the six labs.

Two things deliberately *not* changed:

- **The 0→300/day normalisation ceiling stays.** The comment at that constant says 300
  was chosen so a real engagement surge saturates the family, which is right – the
  ceiling was never the bug. It should be revisited after a week of measurements from
  the live basket, since every previous reading came from the dead one.
- **The velocity mechanism stays.** It already measures only repos present in both
  snapshots, so a basket swap cannot sign-flip the rate; the first run after the swap
  simply rebaselines and measures from the next one. The client-side copy in the page
  did *not* have that guard – it differenced basket totals – so it was brought in line.

## 2. Flagship pricing – pin the line, resolve the version

`PRICE_CN` / `PRICE_US` pinned exact model slugs, and had rotted in both directions:

- `google/gemini-3-pro`, `google/gemini-3.1-pro` and `qwen/qwen3.6-max` no longer resolve
  at all, and silently left the median rather than erroring.
- `anthropic/claude-opus-4.8` stayed pinned while **Opus 5** shipped (2026-07-24).
  `google/gemini-3.6-flash` and `deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash-0731` ($0.18/M, shipped
  2026-07-31) were missing entirely.

Worse than the staleness: the panel's own note says *"falling ratios mean US pricing power
is holding, rising means compression is biting"* – but a fixed basket only moves when a
human edits the file. The metric could not detect the launch-driven compression it exists
to measure.

Now `PRICE_FAMILIES_CN` / `_US` pin the model **line** by regex and `resolveFamilies()`
picks the newest live version at page load. Version numbers turn over monthly; which line
is a lab's flagship turns over every few years, so this pins the stable thing.

**Lines are matched on name, never on price.** Ranking by cost is circular here: the
page's whole thesis is that Chinese labs ship frontier quality cheap, so "most expensive =
flagship" picks DeepSeek's *previous* generation (`v3.1-terminus` $1.00 over `v4-pro`
$0.87). Recency-within-a-named-line is the only non-circular rule available. Selecting by
routed volume was also tried and rejected – it picks `gpt-oss-120b` as OpenAI's flagship.

Sanity check that this is a faithful replacement and not a redefinition: against the live
catalogue all 13 lines resolve, zero missing, and the gap comes out at **10x – identical
to the hardcoded basket it replaces**, while now including Opus 5 and V4-Flash-0731.

A line that stops resolving is now named in the panel note instead of quietly dropping out
of the median.

## 3. Prediction markets – ids rot, queries don't

Three of the eight pinned Polymarket ids (`2431240`, `2431245`, `2431244` – the "best
Chinese AI model at end of July 2026" set) return **nothing** from the Gamma API. They
resolved and expired. `loadPM()` drops anything it cannot fetch, so the panel had silently
shrunk from eight rows to five with the freshness dot still reading "live" – on the block
the page itself calls "the cleanest forward-looking read in this monitor".

Now: five curated ids remain as the core, and `PM_DISCOVER` backfills from
`public-search?…&events_status=active`, the same mechanism `polymarket-ai-index.html`
already uses. Discovery found the successors to all three dead rows (the end-of-August and
end-of-September cohorts).

Raw search is far too broad to use unfiltered – "Chinese AI" returns 173 markets including
the Chinese-Military-Companies list and a full ladder of second/third-best rungs. Three
filters, all tested:

- an `accept` regex limiting to the monthly "best Chinese AI model/company" cohort;
- `PM_JUNK`, which drops the unnamed placeholder outcomes Polymarket seeds every such
  event with – they sit at exactly 50c on zero volume and are not anybody's opinion;
- a $5k volume floor, then top-N by volume.

Discovered rows are tagged `AUTO` in the table so their provenance is visible, and the
caveats note says the block rotates between visits.

## Testing

- `test_update_china_data.py`: 8 new tests for `gh_active_repos` / `github_basket` /
  basket-swap rebaselining. Existing GitHub tests updated for the `GH_REPOS` → `GH_SEED`
  rename and the explicit-basket parameter. **55 pass** in that file, **195 pass** across
  `pytest omen/`.
- `test-china-helpers.mjs` (new, added to the `SUITES` list in `test_regime_explainer.py`
  so the documented `pytest` entry point picks it up): 30 assertions covering both
  selection rules, including the "cheaper-but-newer wins" case and every junk-market
  filter.

**Local gotcha:** there is still no node on this Mac, so the `.mjs` suites skip under
pytest here and only run in CI. They were instead executed against macOS's built-in
JavaScriptCore (`osascript -l JavaScript`), which also served as the `node --check`
parse gate for the page's 524-line inline script. Same for end-to-end verification: the
Browser pane blocks both `file://` and `localhost`, so the shipped selector code was
sliced out of the HTML and run against real OpenRouter and Gamma payloads that way.
`Function.bind.apply` is needed instead of `new Function(...args)` under JSC.

## Not done

- **`ARTIFICIAL_ANALYSIS_API_KEY` and `CF_RADAR_TOKEN` are still unset.** The AA scores
  (51 vs 60, dated 2026-07-12) are now the only genuinely hardcoded *value* still
  rendering, carried from the `MANUAL` dict. `artificial_analysis()` is written and
  working – it needs the free key as a repo secret, no code. Same for the Radar card,
  which stays hidden.
- **The "Why this matters" thesis card** is still static prose (capex $400B/$520B/$57B,
  NVDA China revenue, CNBC anecdotes). Only partly fixable: "60–90% below US flagships" is
  derivable from the live price table directly above it, but there is no hyperscaler-capex
  feed in the repo – `capex-data.json` carries TSMC revenue, SEC issuance and EIA
  capacity, not capex dollars.
- **`CN_AUTHORS` / `US_AUTHORS`** are still hand-maintained sets, and a new lab slug falls
  into "other" and dilutes the headline share. No clean continuously-updated
  country-of-origin source exists for OpenRouter author slugs.
